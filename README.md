## The project in one paragraph

We are building a method that generates prescriptive business advice (e.g., "what should this restaurant fix?") by sampling in a sentence-embedding space using a diffusion model, steered at inference time by gradients from external verifier functions encoding policy compliance, tool-grounded feasibility, and similarity to past cases. The base diffusion model is never retrained when new policies arrive — they enter as additional verifier gradients. This gives us provable guarantees on policy violation rate, modular composition of knowledge sources, and a principled trade-off between cost and quality. Target: ICLR 2027 (deadline ≈ end of September 2026). **Read the team research plan in full before starting.**

### Phase 1 — Scale to embedding dimension (Weeks 2–4)

**Goal:** A working unconditional diffusion model in advice-embedding space (768-dim), then a conditional version that takes issue embeddings.

This is the highest-risk phase. Text-embedding diffusion is harder than 2D toy diffusion and harder than image LDM. If we can't make a base diffusion model converge on advice embeddings, the project doesn't work.

#### 1.1 Architecture design (Week 2)

Move from the MLP denoiser used in the toy task to a transformer-based denoiser. Justified by the dimensionality and the need to condition on a variable-length episodic memory.

**Proposed architecture (subject to your refinement):**

- Input: $a_t \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$, timestep $t$, issue embedding $i \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$, optional pooled episodic context $\bar{\mathcal{E}} \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$.
- Project $a_t, i, \bar{\mathcal{E}}$ to a shared hidden dim $h = 512$ via linear layers.
- Add sinusoidal time embedding (you have this from the screening task).
- Stack of 6–8 transformer blocks (pre-norm, SwiGLU FFN, RMSNorm — all things you already know).
- Cross-attention into the issue + episodic context tokens.
- Output linear projection back to $\mathbb{R}^{768}$ predicting $\epsilon$ or the EDM-style preconditioned target.
- Total parameters: ~50M. Comfortable on a single A6000 for training.

Write a 1-page architecture doc in `docs/diffusion_architecture.md` explaining choices. PhD reviews before you train.

#### 1.2 EDM preconditioning (Week 2)

**This is a non-negotiable.** Karras 2022 ("Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models") demonstrates that proper preconditioning of inputs, outputs, and the loss function makes the difference between models that train and models that don't. Their formulation:

$$
\mathbf{D}_\theta(a; \sigma) = c_{\text{skip}}(\sigma)\, a + c_{\text{out}}(\sigma)\, \mathbf{F}_\theta(c_{\text{in}}(\sigma)\, a,\, c_{\text{noise}}(\sigma))
$$

with $c_{\text{skip}}, c_{\text{out}}, c_{\text{in}}, c_{\text{noise}}$ all functions of $\sigma$ derived to keep the network's input and output at unit scale. Read Karras 2022 §3–§5 carefully and implement this exactly.

Without EDM preconditioning, you'll likely get a model that "trains" (loss goes down) but samples that look like Gaussian noise. Diagnose this if it happens by sampling without guidance and checking that the unconditional distribution matches the data distribution.

#### 1.3 Training data — bootstrap from prior pipeline (Week 2-3)

You need (issue embedding, advice embedding) pairs. Source:

1. Run our prior multi-agent pipeline on a subset of Yelp restaurant reviews (the Infra Student will have the review pipeline ready by W2). This produces (issue, advice) text pairs.
2. Embed both with `all-mpnet-base-v2`.
3. Augment with Llama 3.3 70B generated paraphrases of advice (4 per issue) to increase the conditional distribution's spread.

Target: ~30,000 (issue, advice) pairs for initial training. The Infra Student is responsible for producing this corpus by end of W2.

#### 1.4 Train unconditional first (Week 3)

Before tackling the conditional version, train an *unconditional* diffusion on advice embeddings only. This isolates whether the base modeling works.

- Train for ~50 GPU-hours.
- Sample 1000 advices; embed them back through the same encoder (they'll already be in embedding space); decode some via nearest-neighbor retrieval against the advice bank to sanity-check.
- Measure: do sampled embeddings have similar statistics (norm, dimension-wise variance) to real advice embeddings? Do nearest-neighbor decoded advices read as plausible text?

If unconditional doesn't train, **stop and debug before adding conditioning.** Common failures:
- Loss explodes — schedule miscalibrated; check $\sigma_{\min}, \sigma_{\max}$.
- Loss decreases but samples are unusable — preconditioning likely wrong.
- Samples cluster on a single mode — too small a model or insufficient training.
- Samples don't converge to data marginals — embedding space is non-Gaussian, may need scale normalization first.

#### 1.5 Train conditional (Week 4)

Add the conditioning pathway. Train conditional diffusion on (issue, advice) pairs. Use classifier-free guidance dropout (Ho & Salimans 2022) during training — 10% probability of dropping the issue conditioning — to enable both conditional and unconditional sampling at inference.

Sanity checks:
- Given an issue, do sampled advices retrieve back to *relevant* advices in the bank?
- Do different issues produce different distributions (not just the same generic output)?
- Does the conditional generally beat the unconditional on a held-out (issue, advice) reconstruction task?

**Milestone M1 (end of W4):** Conditional diffusion model trained on advice embeddings. Sampling produces plausible advices when decoded. Architecture doc, training recipe doc, and reproducible config in repo.

### Phase 2 — Verifiers and first guided sampling (Weeks 5–7)

**Goal:** Three verifiers trained and validated independently, then composed into guided sampling. **Week 7 is the project-level go/no-go.**

#### 2.1 Verifier architecture design (Week 5)

Per the team plan §8.2, three verifiers with different structure:

**$V_\mathcal{P}$ (policy compliance):** MLP over $(\text{advice embedding}, \text{policy clause embedding})$. 3 hidden layers, ~5M parameters. Output: 3-class softmax (comply / violate / irrelevant). Interpret $\log V_\mathcal{P} = \log p(\text{comply})$. Built-in irrelevance handling matters — the verifier must not give strong gradients when the policy clause is unrelated to the advice.

**$V_\tau$ (tool feasibility):** MLP over $(\text{advice embedding}, \text{tool-output embedding})$. Output: feasibility score $\in (0, 1)$. Use sigmoid output. $\log V_\tau$ defined directly.

**$V_\mathcal{E}$ (episodic):** Parameter-free. Given retrieved past cases $\{(a_j, s_j)\}$ with outcome scores $s_j$, define $V_\mathcal{E}(a) = \exp(\beta \cdot \text{cos-sim}(a, \bar{a}_+))$ where $\bar{a}_+$ is the score-weighted centroid of past-case embeddings. $\log V_\mathcal{E}$ is then a scaled cosine similarity, with closed-form gradient.

Write `docs/verifier_architectures.md` covering all three. PhD reviews.

#### 2.2 Verifier training and validation (Week 6)

Train each verifier independently. The Infra Student delivers training data by start of W6.

**$V_\mathcal{P}$ training:**
- 50,000 synthetic (advice, policy, label) triples for US restaurants + hospitality.
- 5-fold cross-validation. Train with cross-entropy loss, balanced classes.
- **Target: held-out validation AUC ≥ 0.85 on the 3-class problem.** If below 0.80, this is a project-level red flag — investigate data quality with the Infra Student, possibly regenerate.
- Run *independent* evaluation on the ~500 human-validated examples (when available — Phase 3). Report human-vs-synthetic accuracy gap.

**$V_\tau$ training:** Similar protocol. Smaller dataset (~20k). Regression target.

**$V_\mathcal{E}$ validation:** Parameter-free, so no training; instead validate retrieval quality. Given a held-out (issue, advice with positive outcome) pair, does the retrieval find related past cases?

**Verifier-level decisions to make in W6, document choices:**
- Whether to expose the verifier's gradient via autograd (slow, flexible) or to derive an analytical surrogate (fast). For the small MLPs we use, autograd is fine — but you must verify timing is acceptable for the 100s of guidance steps per sample.
- Whether $V_\mathcal{P}$ should be evaluated against a single policy clause or against the top-k retrieved clauses (aggregated). Strong preference: aggregate top-3 retrieved clauses to avoid brittleness to retrieval errors.

#### 3.1 Manifold-projected guidance (Week 8)

Implement the projection of verifier gradients onto the tangent space of the learned data manifold, evaluated at the Tweedie posterior mean.

**Algorithm:**

1. At reverse step $t$, compute Tweedie posterior mean
   $$
   \hat{a}_0 = \frac{a_t + \sigma_t^2\, s_\theta(a_t, t \mid c)}{\sqrt{1 + \sigma_t^2}}
   $$
   (exact form depends on your VP-vs-VE parameterization — derive carefully).

2. Compute the Jacobian-vector product $J = \nabla_{a_t} \hat{a}_0(a_t)$. This needs **one JVP call** — use `torch.func.jvp` or `torch.autograd.functional.jvp`. Do not compute the full Jacobian.

3. Compute the verifier gradient $g = \nabla_{a_t} \log V_k(a_t, \cdot)$.

4. Project: in the small-noise regime, the rows of $J$ approximate a basis for the tangent space. For efficient projection, use the top-$r$ left singular vectors of $J$ (random SVD or truncated SVD; $r = 32$ is a reasonable default).

5. Replace $g$ with $\text{Proj}_{T_{\hat{a}_0}(\mathcal{M})}[g]$ in the guidance update.

**Validation:**

- Toy: re-run the 2D 8-Gaussian task with both unprojected and projected guidance. At high $w$, unprojected guidance should push samples off the data manifold (between modes, or outside the circle); projected guidance should keep samples on-manifold. This produces a clean ablation figure for the paper.
- Quantify: under high $w$, mean distance from samples to the nearest data-mode centroid. Projected should be lower.

**Open implementation questions to resolve in W8:**

- Does the projection cost (one JVP per step) justify the quality gain on advice embeddings? Measure carefully.
- Does projection help at all $\sigma_t$, or mainly at small $\sigma_t$? Hypothesis: at large $\sigma_t$, the manifold concept is poorly defined and projection adds noise; at small $\sigma_t$, projection corrects gradient direction. Either way, the adaptive schedule (next) should incorporate this.

#### 3.2 Adaptive guidance schedule (Week 8–9)

Implement
$$
w_k(t) \propto \frac{1}{\sigma_t^2 + \lambda_k}
$$
normalized to a total guidance budget $W = \sum_k \int_0^T w_k(t) \, dt$.

The $\lambda_k$ are per-verifier hyperparameters tuned on the validation set. Intuition: small $\lambda_k$ → strong guidance at all noise levels; large $\lambda_k$ → guidance concentrated at low noise (where the verifier is more reliable).

**Ablation:**

- Compare against three fixed-weight baselines (small, medium, large constant $w$).
- Compare against a "naïve scheduled" baseline that just decreases linearly with $t$.
- Sweep $\lambda_k$ on a coarse grid; pick best on validation.

#### 3.3 Multi-source composition (Week 9)

**This is where you need to redeem the screening task's missed conflict experiment.**

Plan, **before running anything**, a 2D toy experiment that *exposes* gradient conflicts between two verifiers. Concretely:
- $V_1$ prefers right half-plane (HalfPlane verifier, your existing).
- $V_2$ prefers the top-left mode (TargetPoint verifier at $(-1.4, 1.4)$).

These two verifiers genuinely conflict — there is no point that satisfies both well.

Run a grid $(w_1, w_2) \in \{0, 1, 3, 10\}^2$. Measure compliance with $V_1$, compliance with $V_2$, sample diversity, and "consensus" compliance (an intersection metric).

Run the experiment unprojected. Then run it projected. Then run it with the adaptive schedule. The expected pattern: naïve composition fails in the predictable ways your Q4 writeup described; manifold projection helps somewhat; adaptive schedule helps further; together they handle the conflict gracefully.

This experiment becomes Figure 4 of the paper. Make it clean.

## Hard problems you will hit (and what to do)

**Problem: Unconditional diffusion doesn't converge on advice embeddings.**
*Action:* Diagnose in order: (1) is the data normalized? Sentence-transformer embeddings have unit norm; check that EDM's $c_{\text{in}}$ handles this. (2) Is $\sigma_{\max}$ large enough relative to embedding scale? (3) Is the model architecture too small? Try doubling width. (4) Is the loss weighting per Karras 2022 §5 actually being applied? Print weighted vs unweighted loss separately.

**Problem: Conditional diffusion ignores the issue conditioning.**
*Action:* Check classifier-free guidance dropout rate (should be 10%, not 50%). Check that the conditioning is actually entering the network (visualize attention or use a probing classifier on hidden states). Increase issue-conditioning channels in the network.

**Problem: $V_\mathcal{P}$ trains to ~70% AUC and won't go higher.**
*Action:* Likely a synthetic-data quality issue. Talk to Infra Student about regenerating with stricter self-consistency filtering. Validate that the irrelevance class isn't dominating. Also try training only on the human-validated subset to see what ceiling is achievable with clean labels.

**Problem: Tweedie posterior mean is unstable at high $\sigma_t$.**
*Action:* Clip Tweedie outputs to $\|\hat{a}_0\| \leq $ a reasonable bound (e.g., 1.5× the max norm of training data). At very high $\sigma_t$, the posterior mean is approximately zero anyway, so guidance from there isn't useful — combine with the adaptive schedule that down-weights guidance at high $\sigma_t$.

**Problem: Manifold projection via JVP is slow.**
*Action:* Profile first. Often the JVP itself is fast; the bottleneck is the SVD. Use random SVD (e.g., `torch.svd_lowrank`). If still slow, project only every 4th step rather than every step.

**Problem: Gradient conflicts between verifiers produce poor compositions.**
*Action:* This is expected from your Q4 analysis. Mitigations in order: (1) manifold projection helps because conflicts often resolve once gradients are projected to relevant subspace. (2) Implement a per-step disagreement check: if cosine similarity between $\nabla \log V_p$ and $\nabla \log V_\tau$ is below threshold, down-weight one of them this step. (3) As an ablation, report uncorrected composition vs. corrected.

**Problem: Sample quality degrades when adding more verifiers.**
*Action:* The combined guidance scale ($\sum_k w_k$) may exceed what the manifold can absorb. Reduce per-verifier weights or shift more budget to higher $\sigma_t$ via the adaptive schedule.

**Problem: Decoder (embedding → text) loses faithfulness.**
*Action:* Two parallel decoders are in the plan. Compare retrieval-based (nearest neighbor over advice bank) to learned (T5 fine-tuned). If neither is satisfactory, fall back to using the sampled embedding as a soft prefix into a frozen LLM — describe in §6 Discussion as a deployment choice.

**Problem: The W7 go/no-go demonstration shows only 5 absolute points of PVR reduction, not 15.**
*Action:* Diagnose first — verifier quality, base diffusion conditioning, or guidance mechanism? Run a triage experiment: hand-craft a few high-quality advices, embed them, sample around them with very high $w_p$ — do you get compliant advices? If yes, the bottleneck is the base distribution or conditioning. If no, the verifier is the issue. PhD makes the call after diagnosis.

**Problem: Infra Student is late on data delivery.**
*Action:* You always have a fallback — fewer domains, simpler verifiers, smaller datasets. Maintain your own backup of a smaller (issue, advice) corpus from the prior pipeline so you can keep training even if the production data is delayed.

## Reading list (Weeks 1–2, ~10 hours total)

This is more than the team plan's baseline — you need the technical depth.

**Must-read in order (Week 1):**

1. **Karras et al. 2022 — Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models (EDM).** Sections 3–6 in full. The preconditioning is non-negotiable. ~3 hours.
2. **Chung et al. 2023 — Diffusion Posterior Sampling for General Noisy Inverse Problems.** Understand the Tweedie evaluation and its justification. Your manifold projection uses the same approximation. ~1 hour.
3. **Dhariwal & Nichol 2021 — Classifier guidance.** Refresh from screening task. ~30 min.
4. **Bansal et al. 2023 — Universal Guidance for Diffusion Models.** This is our closest prior work. Read carefully; know how we differ. ~1 hour.

**Must-read in order (Week 2):**

5. **Ho & Salimans 2022 — Classifier-Free Guidance.** For your CFG dropout during training. ~30 min.
6. **Li et al. 2022 — Diffusion-LM Improves Controllable Text Generation.** Text-embedding diffusion, the closest prior text-diffusion work. ~1 hour.
7. **Rombach et al. 2022 — High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models.** The LDM philosophy that justifies sampling in embedding space rather than text space. ~1 hour.
8. **Chen et al. 2023 — Sampling is as easy as learning the score.** This is the proof technique behind Theorem 1. You need to understand it well enough to defend it. ~2 hours.

**Skim later:**

9. **Xie & Gong 2025 (CVPR) — DyMO.** Closest concurrent work on multi-objective diffusion guidance. Know it. ~30 min.
10. **Yu et al. 2023 — FreeDoM.** Training-free energy-guided diffusion. Related. ~30 min.
11. **Lou et al. 2024 — SEDD.** Discrete diffusion LM, baseline B11. ~30 min.
12. **Our prior ACL submission.** Understand the task and the reviewer concerns this paper addresses. ~1 hour.

If you fall behind on the reading, the result is bad architectural choices in Phase 1. Don't fall behind.
